What Are Walk-In X-Rays?
Walk-in X-rays allow patients to attend a medical imaging centre without scheduling an appointment beforehand. Instead of arranging a specific time, you can visit the centre during its operating hours, subject to service availability and any referral requirements.
Walk-in X-rays at Butler provide a convenient option for patients who need X-ray imaging and prefer the flexibility of attending without a pre-booked appointment.
X-rays use a small amount of ionising radiation to create images of structures inside the body, particularly bones. They are commonly used to help healthcare professionals assess injuries, fractures, joint problems, and certain chest conditions.
Your doctor or treating healthcare professional will determine whether an X-ray is appropriate based on your symptoms, medical history, and clinical needs.

What Conditions Can an X-Ray Help Assess?
X-rays are widely used in diagnostic imaging because they can provide clear images of bones and some other internal structures.
Depending on your symptoms and your doctor's referral, an X-ray may help assess:
1. Suspected fractures
If you have fallen or sustained an injury during sport or everyday activities, an X-ray may help identify a broken bone. Common areas examined include the wrist, hand, ankle, foot, arm, and leg.
2. Joint pain and injuries
X-rays can help assess joint alignment, certain bone abnormalities, and changes associated with conditions such as osteoarthritis. Your healthcare professional may recommend further imaging if more detail is needed.
3. Chest conditions
A chest X-ray may be requested to investigate certain respiratory symptoms or assess the lungs and surrounding structures. Your doctor will determine whether this examination is suitable.
4. Bone and joint changes
X-rays may help identify bone spurs, narrowing of joint spaces, and other structural changes that can occur with some musculoskeletal conditions.
Important: An X-ray does not identify every cause of pain or injury. Some soft-tissue problems may require ultrasound, CT, MRI, or another examination, depending on your clinical situation.
